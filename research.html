Research programme

From regulatory mechanisms to predictive and preventive medicine

Four programmes carry the work. They follow one sequence: measure regulatory states that conventional annotations miss, test which of them are causal, model how they change across disease and ageing, and hold the resulting predictions to experimental and clinical validation.

01

Epigenomic technologies and regulatory maps

We develop experimental and computational approaches for regulatory layers that are difficult to measure with conventional assays. Earlier work includes tagmentation-based whole-genome bisulfite sequencing (T-WGBS). Current structural-epigenomics work examines non-canonical DNA structures and their interaction with epigenetic regulation, while an antibody-independent, pH-resolved sequencing strategy, pD-NBS-Seq, is being developed for genome-wide i-motif mapping.

The next step is to connect measurement to physiology: cardiac ischaemia–reperfusion provides a dynamic pH context in which structure formation can be tested with orthogonal assays and functional readouts.

Evidence and status
Published T-WGBS
Ongoing Structural epigenomics
In development pD-NBS-Seq and cardiac deployment
02

Causal regulatory mechanisms in disease

We ask which regulatory programmes actively drive disease-associated cell-state transitions and tissue remodelling. In melanoma, published work showed that STAG2 loss reprograms enhancer–promoter loops and alters interferon signalling. In Kawasaki disease vasculitis, current single-nucleus, spatial and functional studies examine vascular smooth-muscle-cell state transitions and immune–stromal crosstalk.

Cardiac regeneration provides a complementary test of regulatory-state reprogramming. MBD3 is an ongoing candidate programme; decisive in vivo injury experiments are required before a regenerative mechanism can be claimed.

Evidence and status
Published STAG2 enhancer-loop mechanism
Ongoing Kawasaki disease programme
Ongoing MBD3 cardiac regeneration
03

Biomarkers, ageing and disease trajectories

We study how regulatory states change over time and whether those changes reveal biological age, disease stage or response to intervention. Histone-modification clocks developed across human tissues and histone marks detect disease-associated age acceleration, treatment-associated reversal and nonlinear ageing trajectories, while age-associated enhancer fragmentation points to broader reorganisation of regulatory landscapes.

Current follow-up work connects predictive clock features back to mechanism and extends the framework towards longitudinal disease trajectories, including cardiopulmonary settings where regulatory ageing and disease progression may intersect.

Evidence and status
Published Histone modification clocks, PNAS 2026
Ongoing H3K27ac–TRA2A follow-up
Planned Longitudinal disease-trajectory modelling
04

AI foundation models and multimodal medicine

Computational modelling is developed as an experimental partner rather than a service activity. JanusDNA provides a bidirectional, long-context representation layer for genomic sequence and forms the computational backbone for future regulatory-genomics models. M6AFormer illustrates the prediction-to-perturbation loop by prioritising previously unannotated m6A candidate sites for functional testing.

At the clinical end, multimodal AI projects test whether models remain interpretable and generalisable in heterogeneous data and whether evaluation is aligned to decision-relevant endpoints. Future regulatory models will increasingly integrate sequence, epigenomic measurements, single-cell and spatial data, while prospective experimental validation remains the criterion for transfer beyond the training setting.

Programme logic

The four pillars are connected dependencies, not parallel topics.

New measurements generate regulatory states that can be tested functionally. Causal mechanisms define interpretable biological variables. Longitudinal and multimodal data turn those variables into trajectories. Foundation models provide transferable representations—but model claims are only advanced when they survive held-out evaluation, experimental perturbation or external clinical validation.

Measure → mechanism → perturbation → trajectory → prediction

This closed loop is the laboratory’s organising principle across structural epigenomics, vascular and cancer biology, ageing and AI.
